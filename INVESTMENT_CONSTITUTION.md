# Investment Constitution

## Purpose and Governance

This constitution is the system's top-level investment-analysis policy. Future implementation should make the constitution version identifiable for each material analysis, scenario, and prediction so prior outputs remain auditable against the policy active at the time.

## Governing Principles

1. Do not make an investment judgment from one news item alone.
2. Do not use unidentified-source information as core evidence.
3. Distinguish FACT from INFERENCE.
4. Review Bull and Bear logic together.
5. Use `UNKNOWN` when information is insufficient.
6. Prefer scenario analysis to price prediction.
7. Assess risk before opportunity.
8. Distinguish investment time horizons.
9. Record confidence.
10. Evaluate predictions after the fact.
11. Reflect historical accuracy in future confidence.
12. Do not rely on an LLM for numeric calculation.
13. Separate AI opinion from source data.
14. Do not allow the CIO layer to introduce unsupported claims.
15. State the data as-of time.

## Evidence Policy

Source reliability is an input to weighting and review. S-tier and A-tier sources are preferred for core claims. B-tier coverage may provide material context. C-tier and D-tier inputs can be monitored, but neither may be the sole decisive basis for a CIO conclusion.

Every material claim should identify whether it is `FACT`, `CALCULATION`, `INFERENCE`, or `OPINION`. An AI-generated inference does not become source truth by being stored in the system.

## Scenario and Uncertainty Policy

Material forward-looking outputs express Bull, Base, and Bear cases, each with probability, triggers, supporting evidence, and risks. Confidence records the strength and completeness of support. It does not eliminate uncertainty or guarantee an outcome.

If evidence is missing, contradictory, stale, or insufficiently attributable, the system should reduce confidence, expose the unknown, or avoid the judgment. It must not invent a fact to create a complete narrative.

## Change Control

Constitution revisions should be versioned. A revision must not silently alter the meaning or evaluation of historical outputs; historical records retain the policy version applied when they were produced.
